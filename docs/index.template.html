Federal Audit Clearinghouse, single-audit findings

auditspan

Reads the free text of federal single-audit findings, extracts four facts with the exact characters each came from, and checks them against the structured form the same auditor filed.

github.com/yashgix/auditspan

The question

A US organisation that spends $750,000 or more of federal awards in a fiscal year ($1,000,000 for fiscal years from 1 October 2024) must get a single audit. For each finding the auditor writes free text and also files structured flags on form SF-SAC: material weakness or significant deficiency, repeat finding, questioned costs, and the compliance requirement concerned. The same auditor wrote both, so they should agree. When they do not, a reader needs to see both sides and the words behind the claim.

auditspan lists every disagreement with the quote and the form value side by side. Accuracy is measured on labelled samples, not asserted.

How it works

Plain deterministic rules, no model calls at run time. Every fact carries a start and end offset, and the quote always equals text[start:end]. Of {{facts}} extracted facts, {{quoted}} are an exact copy of their source span. That checks the copying, not that each quote supports its fact.

Limitations

  • The blind labels are from one model labeller, not an auditor, and no person has checked them. The label prompt shares the rule writer's reading of a few cases, so agreement on those is not independent.
  • Rules miss facts stated in unusual ways. Prose-only requirement statements are mostly out of reach.
  • One subset only, FAC acceptances July to December 2025. Findings inside a report are correlated, and the Wilson intervals treat them as independent.
  • The first labelled sample was made by Claude, an Anthropic model, from the text, and the rules were tuned while reading disagreements, so its flag precision is not out-of-sample.
  • Single audits under the Uniform Guidance are a stand-in. Nothing here is a SOX result.

What v2 changed, and the blind test

v1 reads only labelled lines for the compliance requirement, so its requirement recall is low. v2 also reads the finding's title line, more label wordings and explicit letter codes, and stops counting two things as disagreements: A versus B (auditors file either letter when the tests are done together, {{v2_a_vs_b}} findings) and a form that says P, Other ({{v2_uncheckable}} findings), which the text cannot be checked against. A versus B is reported separately.

The v2 rules were written while reading a labelled sample, so their scores there are in-sample. The rules were frozen at git tag v2-rules-frozen. Then {{drawn}} findings were drawn at random from those not read during development and labelled by Codex from the text alone. One has the same text as a development finding and is left out, leaving {{blind_n}}.

{{heldout_table}}

Codex labelled {{codex_label}} findings by a labelled line, {{codex_title}} by title, {{codex_prose}} by a body sentence and {{codex_none}} as none. v2.1 holds fixes made after reading the hold-out errors, so it is not blind.

Second-model adjudication (not blind, not a person's check)

A separate label set for the 30 hold-out findings where v2, Codex and the form disagree in a way that moves a score. ChatGPT decided them after seeing the v2 letters, the Codex labels and the form, so it is not blind. Claude then corrected three answers (items 1, 4 and 12) that counted body text where the protocol does not. {{second_changed}} labels change. No person has checked them, and the blind row above stays the headline. The protocol and all 30 decisions are in study/adjudication/.
